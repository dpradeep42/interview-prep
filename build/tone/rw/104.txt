For behavioural questions, I basically use the STAR structure, Situation, Task, Action and Result.

The Situation is one or two sentences of context, just enough to understand the problem. The Task is what I was specifically responsible for. The Action is the longest part, about sixty percent of the answer, and I tell it in the first person, with real technical detail, so it's clear what I personally did, while giving the team credit where it's due. The Result is the outcome with real numbers wherever possible, like time saved, errors reduced, tickets stopped or latency improved, and then what I learned or what changed afterwards.

I keep each story to about two minutes, because longer stories lose the interviewer.

I also keep a small bank of stories that I can adapt to different questions. Like, building the CIRT MCP server, which cut deployments from 6 hours to 2 and from 6 people to 1, works for initiative or innovation questions. The RabbitMQ outage works for production issues and ownership. The CSV bulk import works for delivering a feature end to end. The security fixes work for quality, and mentoring two graduates works for leadership. Having those ready means I can answer clearly instead of searching my memory under pressure, you know.
