X: ACID is basically the set of four guarantees that a reliable database transaction gives you.

X: I'd choose Kafka over REST when the communication is really "something happened" rather than "please give me an answer".
X: With REST, the producer calls each consumer directly, waits for each one, and needs to know who they are. If a consumer is down, the producer must handle it. With Kafka, the producer publishes an event to a topic once, and doesn't know or care who consumes it. Several consumers can read the same events independently, each at its own pace. Kafka also retains events for a configured period, so a consumer that was down catches up from where it left off, a new consumer can replay history, and you can reprocess events after fixing a bug. And it handles very high throughput.
X: So Kafka fits when producers shouldn't wait for consumers, multiple services need the same events, replay and retention matter, or volume is high. REST stays the right choice when the caller needs an immediate answer.
X: For example, shipment status events are needed by inventory, notifications, reporting and audit. Publishing them once to a topic, instead of the shipment service calling four other APIs, removed coupling and made adding a new consumer a non-event for the producer.
