X: A decision I'm proud of was the design of our CSV bulk import for clinical study data. A study here means one clinical trial set up in our IRT system, with its protocol rules, countries and sites, treatment arms, visit schedule, drug kits and user roles.
X: The situation was that study teams wanted to upload large CSV files instead of entering data one record at a time. The key choice was: if some rows are bad, do we save the good rows, a partial import, or nothing at all?
X: I owned the design, so I weighed both options. Partial imports feel friendlier, but in a GxP system they leave a study half-configured, they make rollback and the audit trail messy, and users have to work out what got saved. All-or-nothing is safer, but frustrating if users only find errors at the end.
X: So I proposed a two-step design: first a validate-only preview that checks every row and shows all errors with row numbers, and then an all-or-nothing save in one transaction, but only once the file is clean. I walked the BA and the QA lead through the trade-off and we agreed on it.
X: The result: it's live in production, users fix every error before saving, and we've never had a half-imported study. I learned that the best decision often combines both options. I can go into the technical design too.
